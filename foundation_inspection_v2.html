§ 0.0
Nature of This Report
This report represents the professional inspection opinion of the inspector named herein, prepared in accordance with ASCE Level B evaluation methodology (Texas Section, 2002). The observations, measurements, and recommendations contained within reflect conditions present at the date and time of inspection. This report is intended to inform the property owner of observed conditions and to guide remediation decisions.

No calculations of structural load capacity, soils engineering analysis, or construction code-compliance certification are offered. Opinions expressed herein are professional inspection opinions, exempted from liability under Texas DTPA §17.49(c) as professional services. Where sealed engineering design documents are required by local jurisdiction for remediation, the remediation contractor is responsible for retaining such documents as part of the repair scope.

Sub-Slab Plumbing Impact Assessment

§ 3.6

3.6.1Background & Code Basis

Horizontal sanitary drainage piping must be installed at uniform slope to maintain self-cleansing flow velocity (≥ 2 ft/sec). Differential foundation settlement alters the as-built slope, potentially reducing flow below self-cleansing thresholds, creating back-pitch (reverse slope), or exceeding joint-deflection tolerances — resulting in separations, leaks, and accelerating foundation damage via soil erosion.

IPC Table 704.1 / IRC Table P3005.3 — Minimum Slope by Diameter
  • 2-1/2 in. pipe or smaller — 1/4 in./ft ( 2.083 % grade )
  • 3 in. through 6 in. pipe — 1/8 in./ft ( 1.042 % grade )
  • 8 in. and larger — 1/16 in./ft ( 0.521 % grade )

5.2Deflection & Tilt Calculations

ASCE Level B Definitions

Deflection — Maximum deviation from a straight line between two points divided by the distance between the two points (D/L). Tilt — Planar rotation: total elevation range (rise) divided by shortest distance between highest and lowest points (run), expressed as percentage. Tilts exceeding 1% from original grade in combination with observable damage are generally considered a problem (ASCE Guidelines §5).
